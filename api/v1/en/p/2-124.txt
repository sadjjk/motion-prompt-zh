Motion Prompt #2-124

What it is: Coins drop one by one into one pan of a balance scale, which tips a little further with each coin and sways to rest, showing one side outweighing the other
What the sample shows: A beam on a central post with pans A and B. 5 gold coins fall into pan B one after another and stack up; the beam tips further with each coin, sways with damping, and finally levels out again.
Spec:
- A dark card with a post in the middle and a beam 300 px long pivoting on a gold dot; a pan hangs from each end on 2 cords and stays level while the beam turns. The pans are labelled A and B.
- 5 coins fall into pan B from above the card, the first at 0.6 s and then every 0.45 s; each takes 0.35 s to fall, speeding up, and lands 6 px above the previous one.
- Each coin that lands adds 0.05 rad of tilt, eased in as 1 − e^(−4t)·cos(10t) with t the time since it landed, so the beam dips a little past each new angle and settles.
- At 5.4–6.0 s the coins fade out and the beam eases back to level; at 0 s the pans are empty and level, so the loop restarts cleanly.
When to use it: Use it when comparing two options, costs against gains or priorities, and you want to show one side winning.

Motion Prompt #2-127

What it is: An exponential curve that stays almost flat at first and then shoots up, drawn to show how small gains add up
What the sample shows: On left and bottom axes, the growth curve for 10% per period over 40 periods is drawn from the origin. A white dot rides the tip to the right, the curve shoots up near the end, and 「×45」 appears at the top right.
Spec:
- Grey axes frame a plot area 460 × 150 px; the curve is y = (1.1^(40x) − 1) / (1.1^40 − 1), the growth of 10% per period over 40 periods scaled to the plot.
- At 0.3–3.6 s the gold line, 3 px wide with a soft glow, is drawn from left to right with ease-in-out; a white dot of radius 5 px sits at its tip.
- At 3.6–4.0 s the label 「×45」 fades in at the top right in 30 px bold gold, since 1.1^40 ≈ 45.
- At 5.4–6.0 s the curve and label fade out; at 0 s only the axes are drawn, so the loop restarts cleanly.
When to use it: Use it to explain compound interest, habits or learning, where small steps add up to a big difference later.

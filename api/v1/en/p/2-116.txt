Motion Prompt #2-116

What it is: Snowflakes of different sizes and speeds drift down with real depth
What the sample shows: On a night street with a navy sky, building silhouettes and a snowy road, snow falls without a break. Far flakes are small and crisp and fall slowly, middle flakes are a bit bigger and faster, and near flakes are large, faint out-of-focus bokeh that cross quickly. Every flake sways gently side to side as it falls. Flakes passing through the street lamp's cone of light turn warm white and look a little bigger, and the light pools on the ground.
Spec:
- 3 layers: 70 far flakes (0.7–1.3 px radius, 0.75 opacity), 36 middle flakes (1.6–2.4 px, 0.85) and 9 near flakes (6–10 px soft bokeh, 0.32).
- In 6.4 s the far layer falls 1 screen, the middle 2 and the near 3, so nearer flakes are faster and the loop has no seam.
- Side-to-side sway is 6 px far, 12 px middle and 22 px near, swinging 1 or 2 times per 6.4 s.
- The lamp head is at (392, 70); its cone widens to a 118 px half-width at the ground (y = 186), and flakes inside turn warm white and grow by up to 35%.
- Far and middle flakes are not drawn below the ground. There is no reset segment; the snow keeps falling.
When to use it: Use it for winter scenes, seasonal or holiday moods, or whenever you need slow, continuous background motion.
